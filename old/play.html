Running QX Orb

With Python installed: python app.py (hardware connected), python app.py test (no hardware), or python app.py --invert-y-axis (left-handed Orbs). Then open the URL Flask prints — usually http://127.0.0.1:5000. Full setup notes live in the repo.

Pick your script length

From thirty seconds to a class

The festival pitch

30 seconds
"This ball shows the state of a single qubit. A normal bit is 0 or 1 — arrow up or arrow down. A qubit can also point anywhere in between. Watch what happens when I press this button."

Press H. The arrow moves to the equator. "That's a superposition." Then offer the controller — the moment they press it themselves, you have them.

The booth demo

2 minutes
  1. Start from arrow-up = |0⟩. Press X — the arrow flips to |1⟩. "That's a classical NOT."
  2. Reset. Press H — the arrow goes to the equator. "Superposition: equal parts 0 and 1."
  3. Press H again — it returns to |0⟩. "Gates are reversible. That's part of what makes quantum different."
  4. Hand over the controller and start Level 1.

The classroom session

5–15 minutes

Run Levels 1–4 in order, pausing between each to explain the new gate. Levels 3 and 4 are the moment to show non-commutativity: have a volunteer try H then Z and Z then H, and watch the arrow land in different places. See the teaching guide for a full lesson plan.

Questions you'll get

Quick answers

"Is this a real quantum computer?"

No — it's a visualiser. It shows what one qubit's state would do under these gates. A real qubit you can't look at without collapsing it.

"What about measurement?"

That's the part the Orb doesn't show. Mime it: "if I measured now, with the arrow here, I'd get 0 with probability…"

"Why only one qubit?"

Two qubits already need a four-dimensional space — there's no nice ball for that. The Orb is deliberately the simplest case.

"Can it entangle?"

No — one qubit can't entangle with itself.

What to bring

  • The Orb and its stand
  • The controller (plus a spare)
  • A laptop with app.py tested that morning
  • A long-enough USB-C cable
  • One printed Bloch sphere with axes labelled
  • Checked battery & cable — a dead Orb is a sad Orb
